Do Starbucks prices move with local cost levels?

This map joins public Starbucks Caffè Latte delivery-menu observations with BEA metro-area Regional Price Parities. The goal is not to claim a perfect official price index; it is to show a reproducible way to test whether city-level menu prices line up with local cost pressures.

What the pipeline does now

  • Curates city-level Caffè Latte price observations with source URLs
  • Downloads BEA MARPP data and joins 2024 metro price indexes
  • Models latte price against all-items and housing RPP
  • Maps residuals: cities above or below local-cost expectation

Price vs. local price level

If local costs fully explained Starbucks price variation, points would cluster tightly around the fitted line. Here the relationship is positive but noisy, which is exactly the kind of decision-useful signal a pricing analyst would investigate next.

Data sources and limits

This is intentionally source-transparent. The stronger production version would collect multiple stores per city from one consistent channel, then separate delivery markup from in-store pricing.

Built as a Starbucks Data Scientist proof-of-work: public data sourcing, data quality judgment, city-level feature joins, model residuals, and an interactive decision map.
